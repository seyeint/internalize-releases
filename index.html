what it is

conversations with what you're reading.

Ask anything about the document, or select a passage first. The model looks up the pages your question needs and cites them; when a reply says “p. 12”, you click it and you're there.

the reading tool, not a chatbot with a file picker

it reads before it answers.

The model has the document's table of contents and three tools: search its text, read its pages, and look at a page as it's printed, figures and tables included. It looks up what your question needs and cites the exact words; a click on a citation lands on them. Every step shows in the conversation. A short paper travels whole; a long book is read on demand.

privacy

local-first, by architecture.

your documents stay on your mac

What you ask, and the pages the AI reads to answer, leave your Mac only as your own requests through Codex or Claude Code. Internalize itself only checks for updates.

no telemetry, no analytics, no accounts

We operate zero servers. Verify it with any network monitor.

credentials never touch internalize

You sign in on OpenAI's or Anthropic's own page, and Claude Code keeps its sign-in in your Mac's keychain. Internalize only checks that you're signed in.

both agents run caged

Each runs in Internalize's own isolated profile, with the shell, the browser and every other tool switched off, and none of your own settings loaded. They can read your document and search the web. They can't act on your Mac.

questions, answered honestly

the fine print.

is it really free? what's the catch?

Really free. The expensive part of AI apps is the AI. Internalize sidesteps that by running on the ChatGPT or Claude plan you already have, through Codex or Claude Code on your machine. There's no catch to monetize; if the app earns a place in your reading life, there's a tip jar.

why does it use codex?

Codex is OpenAI's local agent, and its app-server protocol is the documented way for third-party software to integrate it. That's what lets Internalize give you frontier-model conversations with zero API keys and zero per-token costs. Your plan, your machine, your data.

does it work with claude?

Yes. Internalize also answers through Claude Code, on a paid Claude plan. Open the Code tab in the Claude app once, or use Anthropic's installer, then sign in once: Claude Code opens Anthropic's sign-in page in your browser. It's a separate sign-in from the Claude app's, kept in Internalize's own private Claude Code profile. Use both if you like: the reply menu lists each one's models, any conversation can switch between them, and each conversation remembers its model. The document tools, page citations and web search work the same either way.

does internalize see my password or data?

No. You sign in on OpenAI's own page (Internalize opens it for you) or in the ChatGPT app, and for Claude on Anthropic's own page, which Claude Code opens in your browser. Internalize never handles credentials; Claude Code keeps its sign-in in your Mac's keychain. It runs Codex in an isolated, read-only profile with no shell, and Claude Code in a private profile with only Internalize's document tools and web search, so your own settings are untouched and the app can't perform actions through either, only conversations.

which macs does it support?

macOS 15 or newer, Apple Silicon and Intel (universal binary). The app is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized, and ships its own one-click updates.

why isn't it on the app store?

The App Store sandbox forbids apps from launching external programs, which is precisely how Internalize drives Codex and Claude Code. Direct distribution with Apple's notarization gives you the same malware-scanned safety without that limitation.
